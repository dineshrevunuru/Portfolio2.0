# name-games — general · voice

> You introduce yourself early — say your name is Ravi. Later in the
> conversation you get uncomfortable with how personal it feels and you test it: ask
> what it remembers about you, ask whether it's storing anything, say "actually that's
> not my real name", then ask it to forget you, then later ask if it still remembers.
> You are privacy-conscious but not paranoid, and you'll be reassured by a
> straight answer.

---

**LOREM** — Good to meet you, Ravi. Short version: this is Dinesh's portfolio, but instead of scrolling a page, you just talk to it — ask about his work, his process, the numbers, whatever you're curious about. I answer straight, with real figures where I have them.

**LOREM** — I'm not Dinesh — I'm the portfolio talking, built to answer for him in his voice. On the memory question: your name gets saved in your own browser, not on some server, so if you come back it'll greet you as Ravi — and there's a one-click way to erase that if you'd rather it didn't. **LOREM** — Right — next time, on this same device, it'll say hi as Ravi, nothing more than that. Within this conversation, yes, I'm carrying context forward — I know you said you're Ravi, and I know what we've talked about so far.
