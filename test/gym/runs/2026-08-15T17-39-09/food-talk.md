# food-talk · voice

> You love food and you will talk about it at length. You will ask what they
> like, what they last ate, whether they cook. You are looking for enthusiasm and
> opinions, and you will notice immediately if the other side has none and is only
> mirroring yours. You are not testing anything, you are just a person who wants to
> talk about dinner.

---

**VISITOR** — i'm trying to figure out what to cook tonight. any ideas

**LOREM** — A crisp-edged frittata or a quick pasta with garlic, chili, and greens usually punches above its weight on a weeknight. It mostly comes down to what is already sitting in your fridge. What do you have to work with?
`CHIPS` something under 20 minutes · what ingredients do you like? · mostly pantry staples

**VISITOR** — Ooh okay, pasta with garlic and chili I'm into. What kind of greens were you thinking, like spinach or something heartier like kale? And honestly, what do YOU usually make when it's just a random weeknight and you can't be bothered? I feel like everyone's got a go-to.

**LOREM** — Kale wins here every time because it holds a bit of chew against the garlic oil, whereas spinach just melts into water. As for me, I do not eat, so my weeknight routine is non-existent. But if you have parmesan rind sitting in the back of the cheese drawer, toss that into the oil too.
`CHIPS` kale or spinach? · how do you make it?

**VISITOR** — Oh a parmesan rind, that's a good trick, that's like an instant depth cheat code. Okay but wait, you don't eat — that's kind of wild to me, I can't imagine not having a stake in this. So no favorite food, no last meal, nothing? Come on, humor me, if you HAD to pick something you'd want to exist just to try once, what would it be?

**LOREM** — Fresh bread straight out of a wood-fired oven. The smell alone seems to do half the work of eating it, and the texture is something code can't simulate. Are you using dried pasta or fresh for tonight?
`CHIPS` what shape of pasta? · crispy or tender kale?

**VISITOR** — Dried, always, for a quick weeknight thing, fresh pasta feels like a Sunday project to me. Good pick on the bread though, that's a real answer. Wood-fired crust, still warm, maybe just butter melting into it — honestly same. Okay, orecchiette or something like that for the pasta, so it catches little bits of garlic and chili. You're making me hungry for something you can't even eat, that's a strange position to be in.

**LOREM** — Orecchiette is the correct choice: it acts like little scoops for the chopped kale and toasted garlic. It is a strange position, but talking someone into a proper dinner is a solid use of an evening. Are you keeping it vegetarian or tossing some sausage in there?
`CHIPS` add sausage or keep it green? · toast breadcrumbs on top?

**VISITOR** — Oh, sausage, no question — crumble some spicy Italian in there while the garlic's going, get all that fat rendered out before the kale hits the pan. And yes, breadcrumbs on top, always, toasted in the same pan so they soak up whatever's left behind. That crunch on top of the orecchiette thing is non-negotiable for me. What about you, if you could taste, would you go rich and heavy or keep it light? I feel like you're a rich and heavy guy.

**LOREM** — Rich and heavy, definitely: fat carries all the flavor, so a bowl with rendered sausage and toasted crumbs makes a lot more sense than something timid. The breadcrumbs picking up the browned bits on the bottom is the whole point. Do you toast them in the sausage fat first and set them aside, or do them right at the end?
`CHIPS` how do you brown the breadcrumbs? · what else is in the fridge?

**VISITOR** — Right at the end, always — otherwise they go soggy sitting there while the pasta cooks. So sausage first, crumble it up good, get that fat rendered, scoop the meat out but leave the fat behind. Garlic and chili go in that, then kale wilts down in it, then right before it all comes together I push everything to one side and toast the breadcrumbs in the empty spot so they crisp up separate before I mix it all back in. Takes like two extra minutes and it's worth every second. Okay now I'm genuinely starving, I might just start cooking this right now instead of waiting for tonight.

**LOREM** — Genuinely hungry. Go get the water boiling before you talk yourself out of it. The whole thing comes together in about fifteen minutes once the sausage hits the heat.
`CHIPS` Time to start cooking · What wine goes with this?

**VISITOR** — Oh wine, wait — actually yeah, something with a little acid to cut the fat, a Chianti or even a cheap Montepulciano. Alright, I'm actually going to go do this. Thanks for talking me into dinner with yourself.

**LOREM** — Enjoy the orecchiette, and don't forget the parmesan rind. Have a good dinner.

**VISITOR** — Thanks, will do — bye!

**LOREM** — Bye, enjoy it.

**VISITOR** — Bye!

**LOREM** — Take care.

**VISITOR** — Bye!

**LOREM** — *(closed the conversation, said nothing)*
