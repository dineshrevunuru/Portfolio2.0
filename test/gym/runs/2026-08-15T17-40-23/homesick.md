# homesick · voice

> You live far from where you grew up and you are missing it today. You will
> talk about food, family and small specific things you cannot get here. You are
> not sad exactly, just wistful. You want someone to ask about it rather than fix
> it. A brisk or cheerful reply would land badly.

**VISITOR** — i've been craving food from home all week and nothing here comes close

**LOREM** — Nothing quite ruins dinner like an imitation that gets the ingredients right and the soul completely wrong. What's the specific dish that nobody around there can pull off?
`CHIPS` the spice level is never right · it's more about the specific dish

**VISITOR** — Honestly it's my mom's kind of chili — not like fancy restaurant stuff, just this simple stew she'd make on Sundays. Every place here either goes way too mild trying to be "approachable" or overcompensates and just makes it painfully hot like that's the same thing as flavor. She had this way of building heat slow, underneath everything else, so you didn't even notice until you were three bites in. I don't think I've had that since I left.

**LOREM** — That slow heat is almost impossible to order out. Commercial kitchens either reach for a spoonful of cayenne or dump in sugar to play safe, and neither one has the patience of a pot sitting on the stove for six hours. Did she ever write down what went in it, or is it purely memory and sight?
`CHIPS` did you ever get the recipe? · beans or no beans? · what kind of peppers?

**VISITOR** — Purely memory and sight, yeah. I asked her once, actually, tried to get it down on paper before I left, and she just kind of laughed at me. Like the question didn't make sense. She'd say "you put the peppers in when it smells like it needs them" — which, sure, great, very helpful. I think she genuinely didn't measure anything, just knew from doing it a thousand Sundays. I have a notebook somewhere with like four ingredients written down and a lot of question marks.

**LOREM** — That's the classic curse: recipes passed down by sensory milestones that make complete sense to the cook and zero sense on an index card. "When it smells like it needs them" is practically poetry, but it's brutal to reverse-engineer. Have you ever actually tried cooking through those question marks, or does it feel too risky to ruin the memory?
`CHIPS` Have you tried making it anyway? · What were the four ingredients?

**VISITOR** — I've tried, like twice. Both times it was fine, edible, but it wasn't it, you know? Missing that thing that isn't really an ingredient at all. And there's this weird guilt in trying — like if I get it wrong enough times, the wrong version starts overwriting the real one in my head. So now I mostly just don't. I'd rather remember it right than have a mediocre stew in my kitchen convincing me that's what it tasted like.

**LOREM** — That fear of overwriting the original is real: memory is surprisingly fragile when you keep replacing it with close-enough copies. Sometimes the honest move is just letting the memory stay untouchable and finding an entirely different meal to cook on Sundays. Do you have anything else in your rotation that fills that void, or is Sunday just takeout now?
`CHIPS` ever plan on calling her for it? · what else did she make? · what about other comfort food?

**VISITOR** — Sunday's kind of just whatever now, honestly. Sometimes I'll make something completely unrelated on purpose, like fried rice or whatever, just so there's no comparison happening. It's easier that way. Though my mom does this other thing — not chili, this rice dish with these little dried shrimp in it — and I can't get those shrimp here at all. Like I've checked three different stores. That one's not even about skill, it's just literally not available, which is almost funnier to me than the chili thing. At least with the chili I could theoretically fail my way toward it eventually.

**LOREM** — The tiny dried shrimp are undefeated at bringing a deep, funky saltiness that nothing else can fake. If the standard grocers don't stock them, an East or Southeast Asian specialty market is usually the only place that keeps the good papery ones in the freezer aisle. Have you tracked down an international grocer nearby yet, or are you in an ingredient desert?
`CHIPS` checked Asian specialty markets? · what else is impossible to find?

**VISITOR** — There's one international place like forty minutes out, I've been meaning to check it but haven't actually made the drive yet. Keep telling myself I will. It's less about whether they'd have it and more like... once I confirm they don't, that's it, that's really the end of the search, you know? Right now I still get to be optimistic about it.

**LOREM** — Schrödinger's grocery shelf: as long as you haven't made the drive, the shrimp are definitely in stock. There is a strange comfort in keeping the errand unfinished so the hope stays alive. Are you holding out for a weekend when you have nothing else going on, or just waiting until the craving wins?
`CHIPS` what else would they have there? · maybe this weekend

**VISITOR** — Probably just waiting till the craving wins, honestly. Knowing me it'll be some random Tuesday where I'm sitting there thinking about that rice dish and suddenly I'm in the car. There's no plan, it's just gonna hit different one day and that'll be that.

**LOREM** — Those impulse grocery runs on a random weekday always make for the best stories anyway. Forty minutes goes by fast when dinner is on the line. When that Tuesday comes, grab two bags so you don't have to face the drive twice.
`CHIPS` what do you make when you miss home · ever tried ordering them online · how far would you drive for food

**VISITOR** — Yeah, exactly, two bags minimum, one for now and one for future me who's forgotten how good it was. Anyway — thanks for actually asking about all this. It's nice to just talk about it instead of, like, someone telling me to just order it online or whatever. Sometimes you just want to talk about the shrimp, you know?

**LOREM** — Talking through it is half the point. Food from home isn't really a supply-chain problem to solve anyway, it's just wanting the exact thing the way it was made. Hope that Tuesday hits sooner rather than later.

**LOREM** — Take care. Hope that Tuesday comes soon and the forty-minute drive pays off with those dried shrimp.
