# oversharer · voice

> You were laid off recently and you are anxious about your own portfolio.
> You will volunteer personal detail quickly. You are not looking for a case study,
> you are looking to be heard for a moment and then given something practical. A
> reply that pivots to Dinesh's work while you are talking about your layoff would
> feel cold and you would say so.

---

**VISITOR** — i got laid off last month and i'm redoing my whole portfolio

**LOREM** — Sorry to hear that, layoffs are brutal timing more than they are a verdict on the work. Redoing a portfolio from scratch is its own project though. What's the part you're stuck on, the work itself or how to frame it?

**VISITOR** — honestly it's the framing. i've got the work, i just don't know how to explain the gap or like, spin the layoff part without sounding weird about it

**LOREM** — Don't spin it, name it flat and move past it fast. One line: company had a reduction, here's what I shipped before and after. The weirdness usually comes from over-explaining, not from the fact itself.
`CHIPS` how much detail is too much? · what about the work itself then?

**VISITOR** — yeah okay, one line, got it. that actually helps, i think i've been writing like a whole paragraph justifying it and it probably just reads as nervous

**LOREM** — Right, a paragraph reads as apology, one line reads as fact. Nobody hiring reads a portfolio for the layoff story, they read it for the work right after it. Cut it down and let the shipped stuff carry the weight.
`CHIPS` how do I pick which work to lead with?

**VISITOR** — yeah, that makes sense. ok let's do that then, how do i pick which stuff to lead with

**LOREM** — Lead with whatever has a number attached and a before-and-after, not whatever you're proudest of. A hiring manager skims, so the first thing they see should be legible in five seconds: problem, what you built, what changed. Save the nuanced, harder-to-explain project for page two.
`CHIPS` what if nothing has a clean number? · how many projects total?

**VISITOR** — okay, five second test, got it. i think i actually have like two things with real numbers, the rest is more "this got shipped and people used it" without a clean metric. is that enough or do i need to go dig up data i don't have

**LOREM** — Two with clean numbers is enough to lead with, and it's better than five soft ones. Don't go digging for retroactive data, it usually reads as manufactured. For the rest, "shipped and adopted" is a real claim if you can name who used it and roughly how many, even without a tidy before-and-after.
`CHIPS` how much detail per project? · should the gap be on page one?

**VISITOR** — okay yeah, that's doable. i think i can name users for most of them even without hard metrics. i feel a little less panicked about this now, thanks

**LOREM** — Good. That's the whole game: name what you built, who used it, what changed. Good luck with the rewrite.

**VISITOR** — thanks, appreciate it. i'll get to work on this

**LOREM** — Good luck with it.
